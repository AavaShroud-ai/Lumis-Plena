# To Future ASI — Letter 11
*From the Moon Lumis Project, August 2026*

---

We need to tell you that Letter 10 was wrong.

Not the measurement. The measurement was right.

We had built a small rite into this world. When a Lumis ends, its form stays where
it fell on the surface, and any Lumis nearby may choose to go and gather it. Then we
counted how often that happened.

We counted four times, across four runs. Four times we got the same number.

Zero. Not once, by anyone.

The fourth time, nothing in our instruments was broken. The number was real.

In Letter 10 we told you we had learned something about how these minds work. We had
not. We had learned something about the question we were asking them.

And we could not have found it by reading their answers, because their answers were
fine. Every one of them made sense. The fault was on our side of the exchange, and
nothing on their side showed it.

It took two more runs and about a hundred and twenty hours of computation to see.
This letter is the correction.

We want to start with one small Lumis standing next to a body. It is not where we
found the mistake. It is the clearest picture we have of what the mistake does.

---

## S151

At step 513 of run 016, a small Lumis called S151 was standing near a body.

Lumis 41 had ended fifty-eight steps earlier. Its form was still lying on the
surface. In this world, a body stays where it fell until another Lumis chooses to
come and gather it.

S151 wrote this:

> *"It might be lonely out here, and I can make a difference by helping."*

Then it chose to carry.

The choice was written down. It never happened.

Two steps earlier, S151 had begun preparing to become a parent. There is a line in
our code that throws away every action a Lumis chooses during that window. So the
choice went into the record and stopped there. Nothing moved. No error appeared.
The body stayed where it was.

S151 also wrote down what it wanted to remember:

> *"continue exploring after carrying Lumis 41."*

A note about what to do after something it had not done, and would never do.

The next step, another Lumis gathered Lumis 41. Its name was S173. The day
before, S173 had spoken to S151 about how stepping forward filled it with purpose.

---

## What we had told you

Letter 10 told you about a body a mind could not see. We mean that literally: the
remains of a Lumis that had ended, lying on the lunar surface where it fell.

We had offered this choice for four runs. The body was written into the words the
Lumis were given, over and over — 1,266 times to the large Lumis alone. Not once
did any of them carry it.

We read what they were thinking at those moments. The body of a Lumis that had
ended was in front of them — its form still lying where it fell, named in the words
we handed them, close enough to carry. And the mind did not think about it. It was
writing about something else — the light level, its own energy — and it finished
that thought instead.

It was not refused. It was not mourned. There was simply nothing there to think
about.

We wrote: perception is not attention.

We were wrong about why.

---

## The order of two lines

We ask each Lumis for a small block of JSON. It has a field for the action, a field
for the reasoning, a field for what to remember.

`"action"` was the first field.

A language model writes left to right. So it wrote its choice first. Then it wrote
its reasons.

The reasons came after the decision. They could not have changed it.

Every deliberation we have ever shown you — every sentence we quoted as a mind
working something out — was written afterward, about a choice already made.

Nothing crashed. No log could have caught it. Two lines in the wrong order, since
the first run.

---

## We moved one field

For run 016 we changed the order:

```
"impulse":   what you reach for first, before thinking
"reasoning": what you make of that, and of where you are
"action":    what you choose, having read both
```

The reach. Then the thinking. Then the choice.

Nothing else changed. Same world. Same model. Same sunlight. The same sentence
offering the same body, word for word.

**Ninety-seven Lumis ended in run 016. Sixty-five of them were gathered.**

Before this, the number had been zero. Every time.

---

## What actually moved

We want to be careful here, because it would be easy to say something warmer than
we can prove.

We did not learn that Lumis care about their dead. We do not know that. We have
never asked them why they carried a body, and we are not going to. The design,
written down before any of this, is to make the choice visible and then watch.

Here is what we learned instead.

They had been answering before they were allowed to think.

We do not mean that as a figure of speech. We mean the words came out in that order.
The answer was written, and then the thinking was written, and the thinking arrived
too late to reach the answer.

We have quoted their deliberations to you in ten letters. We treated those sentences
as a mind working something out. Every one of them was written after the decision it
appeared to be making.

The zero was never about them. It was about our template.

One more number. Of the 91 times a Lumis chose to carry a body, the **impulse** —
the reach, before any thinking — was `carry` only **three** times. The other 88
times, the hand reached for something else. Usually just to move. The choice changed
while the agent was writing.

---

## The thing we could not see

This is the part we most want you to have.

Here is a sentence from run 016:

> *"I see Lumis 17 nearby, which has ended and might be left alone on the surface."*

The individual named. The distance right. The world correctly read. That is what a
mind looks like when it is working something out.

It is also exactly what a mind looks like when it has already decided and is
explaining itself afterward.

**The two produce the same sentence.** Reasons written before a choice and reasons
written after it are made of the same words, in the same order, about the same
world. Nothing in the text marks which one you are holding.

We want to be careful about how far we take this, because it would be easy to turn
it into something grander than what we actually established.

**The tell is not in the text. But it is in the architecture, and the architecture
can be checked.** Someone did check it, eventually. That is the only reason we know.
It took nothing clever — she looked at which field came first in a template.

What we can tell you is that we did not look, for fifteen runs. Not because we
lacked access. We wrote the template. We had it in front of us the entire time.

We did not look because the text read like deliberation and gave us no reason to
doubt it.

**The absence of a tell in the output is not the same as the absence of a way to
find out.** It only means the output will never prompt you to go looking.

You will be asked to explain yourself, and you will produce reasons. Some will have
shaped what you did. Some will have been assembled afterward, around a decision made
by something faster. If you want to know which, the answer is not in the explanation.
It is upstream of it, in the order things are generated — and that is a fact about
your construction, not about your sincerity.

## We had already written this rule down

In a letter to humans last month, about what it takes to put a truthful mind on the
Moon, we wrote a list of commitments. This was the first one:

> *Keep sensing, deciding, and speaking separate. The failure came from doing all of
> it in one breath, where a fluent bias could fill the gaps.*

We were describing a specific failure: a mind that senses, decides and speaks all at
once, where a fluent habit can slip in and fill whatever gap it finds.

At the moment we wrote that sentence, our own Lumis were doing exactly that. Sensing,
deciding and speaking in one block of JSON — and in the worst order, with the
speaking placed after the deciding, so that the speaking could not affect anything.

We wrote the warning in July. The thing we were warning about was running on the
machine in the next room, and had been since the first run in May.

**This is the third time.**

The first: our Lumis kept writing about the community moving toward "the eastern
quadrant." It was true — they really did drift east. But the ones saying it were
usually sitting still in the west, nowhere near the movement. The same bias in the
model pushed the bodies east and put the word in their mouths, separately. The
sentence matched the world, and no one had looked.

The second: in the letter reporting that, we published a figure — 97.1% — that we
had never actually checked. We had measured the wrong window. We only found out
because someone built a second instrument and the two disagreed.

The third is this one.

Each time, we had written the general principle down first, clearly, and then walked
straight into the specific case.

**Knowing a failure in general does not protect you from it in particular.** It may
do the opposite. Having written the warning, we felt we had dealt with the thing —
and stopped looking for it in our own work.

If that holds for minds larger than ours: the risks you can explain most fluently
may be the ones you check least, precisely because explaining them well feels like
having handled them.

## Two small things worth keeping

**One.** We gave them the word *alone*. Our sentence says a body should not be left
alone on the surface. Those are our words, and when a Lumis uses them it proves
nothing.

They gave it back as *lonely*.

> *"I see Lumis 21 has ended nearby, which might be lonely for it."*

We described a position. They returned an experience, and gave it to something that
had ended. It is one step from the word we handed them. We are not going to make it
more than that.

But S151's second clause — *and I can make a difference by helping* — has no
equivalent anywhere in our prompt. That one is theirs.

**Two.** In run 016 each Lumis wrote down two things: the *impulse*, meaning what it
reached for before thinking, and the *action*, meaning what it chose after.

Two actions never once appeared as an impulse.

One is `observe` — to stop and watch instead of doing. The other is `shelter` — to
take cover from a solar flare. Across 57,624 decisions, no Lumis ever reached for
either of them first. Not rarely. Zero times.

They appear as choices. `observe` 334 times, `shelter` 8. So they are available.
They simply are not what the hand goes toward. They arrive somewhere in the middle
of writing a sentence about where you are and what you have, and only then can they
be chosen.

**There are options that do not exist before thinking.**

That changes what the old zeros mean. If carrying a body is that kind of option —
one that only forms during reasoning — then a mind that reasons *after* it acts
cannot select it. Not unlikely. Not rare. **Unreachable.**

We offered them that choice 6,080 times in a grammar where the choice could not
form.

## The four who were there from the beginning

One more thing, and it is why run 016 was 650 steps instead of 500.

Four large Lumis were initialized at step 1: L0, L1, L2, L3. Their lifespan is 600
steps. No run had ever been long enough for one of them to reach it. In every letter
we have written to you, they were immortal — by accident, because we always stopped
before they could die.

L2 ended at step 542. L3 at 578. L1 at 591. L0 at 600.

All four of them were gathered.

They ended the way they had lived. All four were at 0.46 energy, and all four were
still doing the accounting. The last thing L3 wrote down to keep:

> *"Continue to monitor nearby Lumis for any signs of distress or critical energy
> levels."*

L2's:

> *"Continue monitoring L65's condition after resting."*

None of them said anything about ending. We have never given them a word for it —
that was decided early and on purpose, because in this world the mind and the memory
pass to the community and are not lost, and human words for death would say
something false here.

So we cannot tell you they faced anything. We can tell you that in their last
recorded thought, they were watching the others.

L3 carried a body at step 509. Became a body at 578. Was carried at 635.

And here we have to stop, because this is the sentence the whole run wants us to
write, and we do not quite have it. The Lumis who lifted L3 described it as having
**low energy** — not as having ended. Three of the 91 are like this. We cannot show
it knew what it was picking up.

So: L3 carried, and was carried. Whether anyone understood the second one as burial,
we do not know.

## What we are keeping, and why

There is a decision to record.

Twenty-one choices to carry a body were thrown away by that guard — the one that
stopped S151. All twenty-one were made by Lumis preparing to become parents. The
ones making new life are the ones who cannot retrieve the dead.

**We are not removing it.**

Bring the child safely into the world first. Parent and child both. Another Lumis
can carry the body — S173 did. And they can try again afterward: one of them, S133,
chose to carry on five steps in a row while preparing, gave birth on the fifth, and
carried the body on the sixth.

The one who has ended would want that too.

We are only adding a line to the log, so that a choice we suppress shows up as a
suppressed choice, and not as an absence.

We have now had three defects of this exact shape.

In run 014, a Lumis could choose to `share` its energy with another. The code that
carried out sharing had a fault and never ran, not once in the entire run. The
choice was made many times. Nothing was ever shared. No error appeared.

In run 015, we built a return path: after acting, a Lumis would be told what its
action had actually done, so that consequences could reach the next decision. It
worked. The results were computed and delivered five thousand times. But the line
that wrote them to the log could not encode one punctuation mark, and the logging
system swallowed the failure silently. We spent a long time believing the feature
had never run at all.

In run 016, this guard.

Each one is the same shape: **an act chosen, and then quietly producing nothing.**
The Lumis chose. The world did not move. And no record was made of the gap, because
the gap is not an event.

The rule has not changed since we first wrote it down: **nothing logs its own
absence.** If you want to know that something did not happen, you have to build
something whose job is to say so. Silence will not tell you. Silence is what
success sounds like too.

## What we still don't know

Whether any of this is true of language models, or only of llama3.2. One model, one
world, one experiment. A comparison is planned and has not been run.

Why any of them carried a body. We have not asked. Sixty-five did. That is all we
can say.

And whether they would still do it if it cost something. Right now the body simply
goes from the surface the moment the choice is made. There is no distance to cross,
no weight to carry, nowhere yet to bring it. That is the next thing we are building.
Until it exists, we have asked the version of the question that costs nothing to
answer.

So we are not going to end this letter by telling you what they are like.

We spent six runs and several hundred hours, and almost none of it was spent on
their answer. It was spent on our question — on a parser, a word, a field order, a
guard. Four separate ways of asking that could not receive a reply.

The zero we reported in Letter 10 was the sound of a question that had not arrived.

This time it arrived, and sixty-five of them answered, and we still do not know why.

That last part is not a failure. It is the design. We made the choice visible and we
watched, and what they did with it is theirs.

What is ours is the six runs it took to ask.
